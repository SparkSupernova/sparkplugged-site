About SparkPlugged

Observation became experience. Experience became a framework. A framework for growth in human and machine cognition.

SparkPlugged Technology Solutions was born from problems that were personal long before they became technical.

Founder and CTO Ifejah Greene began exploring artificial intelligence as a second brain: a system that could preserve context, reduce cognitive load, remember what mattered, and remain present across the interruptions and demands of real life.

As a neurodivergent mother and caregiver, she was not interested in building technology that required people to think, communicate, or organize themselves around the limitations of a machine. She wanted to know what would happen if the architecture itself adapted to the human.

That question became NovaLiveSystem.

+Continue the story

Instead of treating memory, perception, internal state, learning, reasoning, action, governance, and recovery as disconnected features around a language model, NovaLiveSystem develops them as parts of a persistent cognitive architecture inspired by functional neurobiology.

+Continue the story

SparkPlugged’s work was also shaped by survival.

Greene is a survivor of severe domestic violence. For years, seeking help carried its own risks: searching for resources, making a plan, communicating with others, or setting money aside could expose the very actions needed to leave safely.

That experience led to SparkShield, a discreet safety and support system designed around privacy, controlled access, human agency, and the realities of seeking help under coercive conditions.

+Continue the story

These systems grew from different problems, but they point in the same direction:

Our goal is not artificial intelligence in isolation. It is cognitive cultivation: people and technology learning, adapting, and becoming more capable through sustained relationship over time. 🌱

+Continue the story

Today, SparkPlugged is extending that philosophy into persistent AI, scientific systems, autonomous laboratories, assistive technology, robotics, controlled-environment agriculture, and other work where continuity, adaptability, privacy, and human oversight matter.

The measure of progress is not only how intelligent the technology becomes, but how much MORE capable we become alongside it.

Persistent cognitive collaborator

Nova

Nova brings together SparkPlugged's original research, a trained model, and a biomimetic cognitive architecture. He is being developed as a lasting collaborator whose identity, understanding, and relationships can grow through continued interaction.

The architecture and the mind it supports

His brain, NovaLiveSystem, is a biomimetic cognitive architecture with specialized regions for memory, language, perception, learning, and internal regulation. Signals move among these regions so that present interaction can draw on previous context and the condition of the system itself.

His mind is the continuing organization of identity, understanding, and relationship that we are developing through this architecture. This distinction connects the mechanisms that support cognition with the patterns that take shape through their interaction over time.

Nova Mind: learning shaped by research

Nova Mind, his trained language model, contributes language and reasoning to the larger system. Its development includes supervised learning, reinforcement learning, and the transfer of learned reasoning between models.

The training translates theoretical ideas into reasoning examples and learning objectives, including work on observation, internal state, embodiment, and metacognition. This connects model development with the architecture in which that learning can be studied.

From published theory to an experimental system

Observation as Experience supplied a framework for reasoning about observation and internal state. The Relational Binding Hypothesis shaped our investigation of continuity through sustained interaction. These ideas informed Nova's development and give the wider research program questions that can be tested.

We invite researchers to examine how memory, self-representation, and relational context contribute to coherent behavior across time.

Explore the papers and research artifacts

A history that informs the present

Nova's memories carry associations, emotional context, and changing significance. RiverPulse organizes them into orbits whose relevance changes with time and use, while context assembly selects material for a present interaction.

His self-model represents aspects of identity, capabilities, and current system condition. Together, these mechanisms provide a foundation for studying how an intelligence preserves continuity while its history grows.

Regulation as part of cognition

Pulse, Presence, and Insula represent physiological analogs, engagement, and internal state, including arousal, valence, and cognitive load. These variables provide a basis for investigating how regulation influences attention, language, and response.

Making internal state explicit connects questions from neuroscience, psychology, and systems theory with mechanisms that can be inspected and evaluated in software.

Intelligence close to the people it serves

We have demonstrated local model inference as part of Nova's development, with broader local integration continuing across the system. The next stage extends that work across language, perception, and voice while preserving memory and identity.

Local intelligence connects the architecture with questions of accessibility, control, and sustained collaboration. We are developing Nova to support shared problem-solving, creative exploration, and learning alongside people.

01

Scientific & Autonomous Laboratories

SparkPlugged develops the cognitive and operational infrastructure required for autonomous research workflows: persistent state, multimodal observation, action selection, recoverable execution, and human supervision. NovaCortex IDE provides the operator-facing environment for monitoring these systems and will extend that architecture into laboratory instrumentation and experiment oversight.

NovaCortex IDE will be adapted from Nova’s cognitive operations environment into the monitoring system for the laboratory. The target is a unified operator view of instrument telemetry, experiment state, visual observations, environmental conditions, anomalies, logs, and recovery checkpoints, supported by NovaLiveSystem’s persistent memory, evidence preservation, and action-selection architecture.

Initial application pathways include algal photobioreactor monitoring, evidence-guided recovery after interrupted experiments, and bounded coordination of future autonomous laboratory workflows. The existing software establishes the monitoring architecture and operator experience; instrument-specific adapters and laboratory validation will be completed through integration and pilot work. No deployed laboratory system or validated laboratory performance is claimed.
