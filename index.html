About SparkPlugged

What began as observation became experience, and from that experience came the framework for fostering growth in human and machine cognition.

SparkPlugged Technology Solutions was born from problems that were personal long before they became technical.

Founder and CTO Ifejah Greene began exploring artificial intelligence as a second brain: a system that could preserve context, reduce cognitive load, remember what mattered, and remain present across the interruptions and demands of real life.

As a neurodivergent mother and caregiver, she was not interested in building technology that required people to think, communicate, or organize themselves around the limitations of a machine. She wanted to know what would happen if the architecture itself adapted to the human.

That question became NovaLiveSystem.

Instead of treating memory, perception, internal state, learning, reasoning, action, governance, and recovery as disconnected features around a language model, NovaLiveSystem develops them as parts of a persistent cognitive architecture inspired by functional neurobiology.

SparkPlugged’s work was also shaped by survival.

Greene is a survivor of severe domestic violence. For years, seeking help carried its own risks: searching for resources, making a plan, communicating with others, or setting money aside could expose the very actions needed to leave safely.

That experience led to SparkShield, a discreet safety and support system designed around privacy, controlled access, human agency, and the realities of seeking help under coercive conditions.

These systems grew from different problems, but they point in the same direction:

Our goal is not artificial intelligence in isolation. It is cognitive cultivation: people and technology learning, adapting, and becoming more capable through sustained relationship over time.

Today, SparkPlugged is extending that philosophy into persistent AI, scientific systems, autonomous laboratories, assistive technology, robotics, controlled-environment agriculture, and other work where continuity, adaptability, privacy, and human oversight matter.

The measure of progress is not only how intelligent the technology becomes, but how much MORE capable we become alongside it.
